Ember Line

Wildfire containment on the real world, cell by cell.

Play it Source All projects

A fire starts at real coordinates in real terrain. You send engines, hand crews, dozers, helicopters and air tankers to cut line, drop water and defend structures for one 90 minute operational period, while the wind shifts underneath you.

No API keys, no backend, no account. Career progress lives in the browser.

The map is the fuel model

Rather than generate terrain from noise, the game classifies fuel by reading the basemap's pixels. CARTO's Voyager style paints water, vegetation, buildings and roads in tightly separated colors, so drawing a tile to an offscreen canvas and counting pixel classes over a cell gives a real land cover read. Lake Tahoe comes back 100% water, Table Mountain 95% timber, central Denver mostly urban.

The lake you can see is the lake the fire stops at, and the subdivision you can see is where the structures are. Classification zoom is fixed and independent of the camera, so a patch of ground always classifies the same way however far in you were when the fire reached it.

How the fire spreads

Every burning cell pushes preheat dose into its unburnt neighbors each second, scaled by wind alignment, dryness, fuel moisture, retardant and any line already cut. A cell ignites when dose crosses its own threshold.

Because spread is certain and the rate controls only its speed, fuel rates are derived from real published rates of spread and hit them: grass around 4.5 km/h, brush around 2, timber around 1.2. Wind dominates on purpose. Head fire alignment is cubed, so a fire under 25 mph reads as a directional run rather than a lopsided blob, and getting ahead of the head becomes the right instinct.

Playing it

Tap a unit, then tap the map to dispatch it. The task is inferred from where you tapped, with line on unburnt ground, direct attack on flame and protection where structures are. The chips above the roster force a specific task, and queue stacks assignments.

  • Cut line ahead of the head and anchor it into something that will not burn, like water, rock or already-black ground. Line cut where the fire was never going is worth nothing, and the containment readout will tell you so.
  • Engines are the only thing that defends structures, covering their cell and the four touching it.
  • Air tankers act on ground that is not burning yet. They are the only resource that can, and they cost more per hour than everything else combined.
  • Keys: space pause, 1 to 4 speed, f fuel layer, esc clear selection.

You are graded on structures lost against the briefed par, how much of the perimeter you held, and what it cost. Acreage is not the score.

Basemap and data credits: CARTO and OpenStreetMap contributors. Incidents are fictional, set on real terrain. They are not reconstructions of any historical fire, and this is not a training tool.
