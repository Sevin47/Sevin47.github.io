Plot Twist: World Deed

A land grab idle game on one shared Earth.

Play it Source All projects

One shared Earth, cut into 300 meter tiles on a real map. You drop a pin anywhere in the world, your street or somebody else's, claim the tile under it, and build out from there. Everyone plays on the same planet, so the map fills up with other people's holdings.

You can play as a guest with no account, or sign in if you want your world to follow you between devices. There is no crypto, no NFTs, no real money and nothing for sale. The currency is a score, and the land is rows in a database.

The loop

  • Claim unowned land. Every tile rolls a deed rarity, and its district, whether downtown, waterfront, urban, coast, suburbs or rural, comes from real OpenStreetMap data, so where you claim genuinely matters.
  • Build it up through cottage, duplex, apartments and tower. Buildings accrue rent while you are away.
  • Trade on an open player market, or flip tiles you would rather not hold.
  • Raid other players to capture their tiles, and defend your own.
  • Collect. Collections, landmarks and contracts give you longer things to chase than a rising number.

The rule that makes it a game

Claiming land costs energy, and you get a fixed amount per day, between 10 and 24 depending on your status rank. That single constraint does most of the design work: money alone cannot sprawl you across the map, so somebody who checks in daily out-claims a much richer player who does not. Rent is idle. Land is not.

Covenants

When a build finishes you draft one of three permanent traits for that tile. Every card is a real tradeoff, something like double defense against a third less rent, and there is no free upgrade anywhere in the deck. Declining is permanent too, for as long as you hold the tile, which keeps the choice weighty and keeps tiles from all converging on the same build.

Running it for nothing

The backend is Supabase and the browser talks to Postgres directly, so there is no game process to keep alive. That is the only reason something like this costs nothing to host.

The grid is a quadkey grid over Web Mercator at zoom 17, which is where the tile size comes from. Ember Line is built on the same grid code, including the per-cell hash that lets the same tile generate the same content for every player forever without storing a single row.
