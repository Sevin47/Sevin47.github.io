Dumb Clanker

Write a program, set your bot loose in the arena, and watch what your instructions actually do.

Play it Source All projects

Every bot in the game is mechanically identical. Nothing to build, nothing to buy, no upgrade tree. The only thing that makes your bot better than anyone else is the program you wrote for it.

I wanted the whole game to be one skill. Any stat you could spend on would have given players somewhere else to look when they lost.

The chassis

One standard body with three things that turn independently. The hull drives and steers. The turret aims wherever you point it, whatever the hull is doing. The radar is a narrow 12 degree beam reaching 38 meters, and it only reports a bot when the beam actually sweeps across one, so a script has to keep it moving or hold it on somebody deliberately.

The arena is a 54 meter square, about 27 bot-widths across, shown in full at all times with start positions randomized every battle. Every bot's radar beam is drawn on the floor in its own color, so you can see where everyone is looking rather than just where you are looking.

Health is a single pool. Firing generates gun heat and you cannot fire again until it cools. Heavier shots hurt more, fly slower and lock the gun for longer, so a power-3 round crawls at 10 m/s against bots that move at 8 and genuinely misses anything that is awake.

Clank Script

Scratch-shaped blocks dragged into stacks, each headed by a "when" hat. when the battle starts is your standing plan and usually wants a forever in it. Every other hat is an interrupt: it takes over, runs to the end, then hands control back where it left off, so two stacks can never fight over the controls.

No block both senses and acts, and nothing aims for you. Conditions are a fixed sensor, comparison and value triple, like if [distance to the target] [is less than] [10].

Sensors in any slot

Every number slot carries a unit, and only sensors measuring that unit can drive it. drive for offers times, turn offers angles, and nonsense like "drive forward for [bots still alive] seconds" is never offered in the first place.

Any slot can take a live sensor reading instead of a fixed number, which is how you build aiming out of parts:

turn radar right [radar: how far off target]keeps the beam on them
turn turret right [turret: how far off target]swings the gun on
if [turret: how far off target] is less than [5]then fire

Each turn corrects once, so a moving target needs re-aiming every lap. Blocks are grouped by what they command: Moving for the hull, then Turret, Radar, Events and Control, with a simple and an advanced palette.

A starter script that loses

The script you begin with wins about two matches in ten. It shows the one pattern worth copying, which is sweep the radar and swing the gun onto whatever it finds, and it does everything else plainly so there is something left to work out. Rival scripts are not readable. If you could open the bot that just beat you, the game would be over in an afternoon.
