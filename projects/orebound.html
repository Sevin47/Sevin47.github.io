Orebound

Dig through a vast pixel world to find the one golden pixel.

Play it Source All projects

You shoot balls into solid rock. They bounce, they break pixels, and every pixel you break is currency for firing more balls. Somewhere out in the world is a single golden one, and finding it ends the run.

The whole game is one HTML file. No build step, no dependencies, no server. You can save it and open it.

Selling impacts, not seconds

Firing a ball costs a flat price and buys a fixed number of impacts rather than a fixed time in the air. That one choice removed the need for a cost curve anywhere in the game. Rock gets harder the further out you erode, so the pixels you get back per shot decay on their own until an upgrade raises your damage again. The board does the balancing.

It also killed a bug I could not see. In an earlier build, travel speed multiplied bounce rate, bounce rate multiplied damage, and damage drove income, so speed was quietly the strongest damage upgrade in the game and nothing said so. Paying for impacts cuts that chain at the first link.

Why upgrades thin out

Upgrade points cannot be bought. They are buried, so their density is the pacing of the whole game, and spreading them evenly does not work. A uniform density scales with area, so the number inside radius r grows as r² and you either drown in upgrades late or starve early. Dropping the density off at 1/d makes the count grow in a straight line with r, so they keep arriving at the same pace however far out you dig.

The price is the only cap

There is no ball limit. Every ball of a color already out there makes the next one of that color dearer, so your swarm settles wherever your income holds it.

The ball I measured wrong

I tuned ball speeds off reference footage frame by frame. One early pass put the purple ball at 2.5 cells per second, and I concluded the game had no fast ball at all.

The tracker only matched a ball to its next position if it had moved less than 90% of one block that frame. Purple crosses several blocks per frame, so the fastest ball in the game was being filtered out of its own measurement. With the cap removed it came back at 58 cells per second, peaking near 70. The same pass caught the balls being drawn about three times too large.
