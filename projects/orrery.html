Orrery

A project tracker drawn as a personal galaxy.

Open it Source All projects

Every project is a planet. Every task you finish becomes a piece of that planet. Everything still open orbits it as dust until you crystallize it, and then the dust spirals in and the planet grows.

The point is to make finished work permanent and visible. Most trackers are built around what is still outstanding, so the reward for completing something is watching it disappear off a list.

Two views

The galaxy view lays planets out on a golden-angle spiral, turning slowly against a starfield of a couple of thousand stars with nebula sprites and a little camera drift. Hovering names a world, and a collapsible index lists them all with their done and total counts.

Selecting one glides the camera in and splits the planet apart into its fragments. Each fragment is one completed task, and clicking it opens what that task was. Leaving the view puts the planet back together.

Stardust and crystallizing

Each open task orbits as its own particle cluster at its own radius, speed and inclination. Clicking one opens the detail panel with a crystallize action. Take it and the dust spirals inward, the planet rebuilds with the new fragment in place, a bloom of light expands and a chime plays. Finishing the last task on a world gets a bigger bloom and a soft three-note chord.

Tasks carry a title, notes, a due date and a status, and can be sent back to stardust if you were premature about it.

Planets generate themselves

Appearance is deterministic from the project id, so a project always looks like itself, with hue in a teal to violet range, latitude banding, polar caps and scattered bright faces that read as cities of light.

The parts that move are the parts that should. Radius grows with completed tasks, an atmosphere glow builds, rings appear at eight crystallized tasks, and a brand new project with nothing done yet renders as a small pulsing molten core.

Calm by default

Serene and cosmic was decided before any code: deep blues, nebulas, ambient sound, no neon and nothing cartoonish. Navigation is click and zoom with the camera gliding, with no ship and no free flight, because free flight turns a calm space into a chore.

Generative ambient audio is available and off until you ask for it. Reduced motion slows the rotation, drops the parallax and skips the dust spiral. Everything saves locally and stays until you delete it.
