Roadworks

A shared, real-time game about keeping the roads open.

Play it Source All projects

You play a county manager. A new work board appears every morning, you dispatch crews to it, and the whole office works the same board. The more crews on a job, the faster it closes.

The bet was that the real data is the game. Every mechanic either makes the daily board more interesting to act on or gives you a reason to come back tomorrow. Anything that could have existed in a generic clicker got cut.

Real and invented

The roads are real, taken from measured public state GIS centerlines, so a work zone sits at genuine milepoints on a road that exists and a crew drives an actual route to reach it. The garages are real locations under invented names, because a crew rolling out of a garage that is really there is the whole appeal. The weather is real, driven by live National Weather Service alerts.

The work itself is invented, placed at generated milepoints from a generic maintenance catalog. Nothing is copied from anyone's published schedule. It is an unofficial hobby project and reads public open data only.

No game server

There is no resident process anywhere in this project. The browser talks to Postgres, every mutation is a security definer procedure, and progress comes from timestamps instead of a tick loop. One scheduled job builds each day's board and another watches the weather. In between, nothing runs.

The crowd multiplier

Effort is counted in crew-seconds, and crews stacked on one job work faster together, up to a cap of triple. Everybody who contributed shares the experience and the budget.

The rule I would not break: no mechanic is allowed to make you unhappy to see another crew show up on your job. Competition happens between districts and never inside a job. Several decent ideas failed that test and got thrown out.

Storm mode

When the weather service has counties under a warning, incidents concentrate there, take their character from the weather and pay double. Winter warnings spawn plow work in priority order, interstates first. This is my favorite part of the project, because the game gets more interesting on days I had nothing to do with.
